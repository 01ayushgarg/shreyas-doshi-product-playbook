# Worked example 02 · A founder session

> ⚠️ **Illustrative.** “Tallyroom” and its people are **fictional**, and the situation and numbers are
> **invented** to show how the founder route in `SKILL.md` works. The frameworks and quotes are real and
> cited (see `SOURCES.md`). All advice not in quotes is our reading.

## What the founder said

> *I'm the solo founder of Tallyroom, B2B expense software for small accounting firms. Six of us, 14 paying
> customers. Pilots love it, but deals stall after the trial with a promise to talk next quarter. A big firm
> wants SSO, audit logs and custom roles before signing. Investors say I need a PM. I decide everything,
> and I'm the bottleneck.*

## The session summary (the skill's output)

# Session: Tallyroom (founder, pre-seed)

**The real problem:** you may be solving a problem, not the problem. Next quarter is the signal in his story:
"that problem was not in the stack rank of priorities and was not a very highly ranked problem." [PVP 02:45]
The bottleneck is a second, related problem: the strategy lives in your head, so every X-or-Y choice comes
back to you. [PVP 34:55]

**Level mismatch?** You're at the impact level; the team is asking execution questions you haven't answered
in writing. [L1 00:47:58]

## Do this week

1. **Stack-rank before you sell.** In your next 8 customer calls, ask for their top three problems before
   mentioning yours. [PVP 02:45] Ask whether Tallyroom prevents the buyer from getting fired, or gets them
   promoted. [PVP 08:10] *(Invented result: expenses rank in the top three for 3 of 8 firms, all of them
   firms of under 10 people with no in-house bookkeeper.)*
2. **Write one page and repeat it.** Who it's for (the segment the calls point to), how you'll win, the
   one sentence a customer would tell a friend [PVP 16:40], and what you'll be below par on. Then be the
   "chief repeating officer" [PVP 34:55]: open every planning meeting with it.
3. **Say no to the table stakes, for now.** The large firm's SSO, audit logs and roles may be real needs,
   but "just by building table stakes, you've not made it any easier for a customer to say yes" [PVP 22:00].
   A smaller, chosen segment makes differentiation possible. [PVP 22:00] Run the reversibility check
   (`templates/09-decision-reversibility-check.md`) before committing: this one is probably a one-way door.
   [L2 23:19]

## The L task you're avoiding

The one-page strategy. "Time becomes a convenient excuse" [L1 00:42:38]. Book half a day somewhere other than
the office. [L1 00:44:26]

## On the PM hire

Not yet. **Our reading:** a PM hired into a missing strategy inherits the deadlock. Once the page exists, write
an MSN list (`templates/06-msn-hiring-list.md`) with mostly intrinsic Must-haves, and decide which decisions
you'll hand over. Prefer "Go Getters over Frustrated Geniuses" [X 2020-06-27, 5/20].
