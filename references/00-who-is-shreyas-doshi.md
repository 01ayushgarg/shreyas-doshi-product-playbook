# 00 · Who Shreyas Doshi is, and how to read him

Citations: `[L1 hh:mm:ss]` and `[L2 mm:ss]` = Lenny's Podcast 2022 and 2024 · `[AMP hh:mm:ss]` = Amplitude
interview, 2021 · `[PVP mm:ss]` = Prime Venture Partners podcast, 2021 · `[SS slug]` = his Substack ·
`[X date]` = his post on X. Full list in `SOURCES.md`.

A product leader best known for turning lessons from Stripe, Twitter, Google and Yahoo into frameworks that
product people actually use: LNO, pre-mortems, the three levels of product work, and the idea that most
execution problems are really strategy problems [L1 00:54:49].

## Career, from his own descriptions

- He started as a software engineer at a couple of startups, then moved into product. [L1 00:07:11]
- Product roles at Yahoo, Google and Twitter, then Stripe, where he led product for Stripe Connect and Stripe
  Terminal and was Stripe's first PM manager (his Maven course page).
- He has managed PMs at each of the four companies where he was a PM, and joined three of them as an
  individual contributor. [AMP 00:05:13]
- He sees himself as "very much as a zero to one and a one to 10 product person" [PVP 02:45], and now
  writes, teaches and advises.

## Where his ideas live

Two Lenny's Podcast episodes (2022, and live in 2024), a 2021 interview with Amplitude, a 2021 founder-focused
podcast with Prime Venture Partners, a free Substack (since December 2025), and years of posts on X
(@shreyas). This repo uses all six. See `SOURCES.md`.

## His own warnings about his advice

He is unusually direct about how not to use him:

- **Don't copy it blindly.** "Don't believe everything I write and don't take everything I write and just
  implement it because you like my writings." [AMP 00:01:07]
- **Context decides.** "Take it with a grain of salt as it may not work for your context always."
  [PVP 02:45]
- **He has been the anti-pattern.** "Many of these anti-patterns that I write about, I have been that
  anti-pattern." [AMP 00:01:07]
- **His base case is an empowered team.** He says he has "largely worked at organizations where the teams
  have just been empowered by default", and that he doesn't know what success looks like where teams are not.
  [AMP 00:30:29]
- **Frameworks are tools.** "Make the framework work for you, and make sure you don't work for the
  framework." [X 2023-07-19](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1681697116027068418)

## Mindset, principles, tactics: the order to learn him in

He splits getting good at anything into three layers: "There's mindset, there's principles, and tactics"
[AMP 00:30:29]. People start with tactics, which is fine, but many "get stuck at tactics for the majority, if
not the entirety, of their career." With principles "you can create your own tactics", and "mindset is the
most important thing." [AMP 00:30:29]

**Our reading, as a way to use this repo:**

| Layer | In this repo | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| Tactics | The templates (`templates/`) | You need to run something this week |
| Principles | The chapters (`references/`) | A template doesn't fit your case and you need to adapt it |
| Mindset | Fear behind L tasks (01), high agency (08), clear thinking (11) | You know what to do and still aren't doing it |

## PM or founder?

Most of his material speaks to PMs and PM leaders inside companies. Founders get the most from chapters 04,
05, 06 and the founder track in `13-for-founders.md`, which pulls together what he says to early-stage teams:
customer problem stack rank, the fired-or-promoted test, minimum lovable product, and the CEO as "chief
repeating officer" [PVP 34:55].

## Limits of this repo

- His paid course and paid posts are not used, so some frameworks he names (for example the Radical
  Delegation Framework) appear here by name only.
- Several of his best-known X threads carry their detail in images, which we can't quote. Where that's the
  case, the chapter says so.
- Wherever a step, number or threshold is ours, it is labelled **Our reading** or **Our suggestion**.
