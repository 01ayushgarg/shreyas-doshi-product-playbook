# 04 · Strategy vs execution

## Most execution problems aren't

> "Most execution problems that I encounter in a high performing environment where everybody has the right
> intentions are actually not execution problems, they are either strategy problems or interpersonal problems
> or cultural problems." [L1 00:54:49]

> "Good leaders execute well because they understand this. They fix the root problem."
> [X 2020-10-15](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1316573473934696449)

| Real cause | What it looks like | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy | "the reason we are misaligned is because we don't know what the strategy is" | [L1 00:57:19] |
| Culture | "you have set a culture that you are supposed to mainly optimize for your OKRs" | [L1 00:58:49] |
| Interpersonal | "It's simply that these two people cannot get along." | [L1 00:59:17] |
| A real execution problem | a skills gap, or aging infrastructure | [L1 01:00:23] |

**The test:** watch for "when you put on a bandaid and the bandaid falls" [L1 01:01:18]. A fix that keeps
falling off means you treated the symptom. And beware: "organizational memories tend to be surprisingly
short." [L1 01:01:49]

He tells founders the same thing. In fast-growth startups, many problems that show up as execution problems
are really about not having a strategy, or one that lives only in the founder's or product head's head:
"So the team doesn't know what the strategy is and so every time they run into should we do X or Y, they
reach this place of deadlock." [PVP 34:55] Decisions then escalate to the CEO, who wonders why the teams
can't be more self-sufficient. His answer: it's a problem the CEO or head of product needs to fix. [PVP 34:55]

## “Strategy” is a misunderstood word

> "Strategy is unfortunately misunderstood and misused word in the valley and many other places that people
> will sometimes create a detailed project plan, and then call it a strategic plan." [PVP 34:55]

> "They'll say, this is my strategy, whereas it's not a strategy but a list of things to do." [PVP 34:55]

What it is instead: "the strategy is really about having clarity on essentially who are you building this
product for and making choices around how you are going to win" [PVP 34:55]. His four-part version on X:

> "Strategy is about: 1) Seeing reality as it is 2) Simulating future realities 3) Creative choice-making
> 4) Tying choices to actions" [X 2021-01-17](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1350614419584139265)

## The formula

> "Impact = (Execution ^ Strategy) × Market" [X 2021-08-22](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1429544548628189186)

**Our reading of the notation:** strategy sits in the exponent and the market is a multiplier, so a weak
strategy caps what execution can deliver, and a small market caps everything. Our sources hold only the
first post of his thread on this formula, so we don't attribute a fuller explanation to him.

## Each document answers one question

His one-line map gives each planning document one question: mission answers why, vision what, strategy how,
segmentation who, positioning where, and roadmap when.
[X 2021-11-17](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1460848932116844550)

## Do you have one? The litmus test

> "When your team is making prioritization decisions with high/medium impact, how often does the discussion
> reference the strategy? If it happens rarely or never, you don't have a strategy."
> [X 2021-08-09](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1424802272727355401)

## Writing it isn't enough: the chief repeating officer

He learned “the hard way” that presenting the strategy once doesn't land: people forget, and in a growing
company new people join. [PVP 34:55]

> "It's really the chief repeating officer role." [PVP 34:55]

Only once the strategy is rigorous and understood do prioritization frameworks help: "the frameworks by
themselves are not very useful without having a strategy in place." [PVP 34:55]

## What a real strategy buys you

> "If you have a real product strategy, a real one that everybody is aligned with, that you have got
> pre-alignment on, then a lot of this nonsense we tend to do with annual planning actually goes away."
> [L2 15:27]

"You don't need that false precision." [L2 15:27] And: "Most product prioritization problems are really
strategy problems." [X 2020-10-24](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1320105222522376192) Teams that skip strategy
work end up "litigating (and re-litigating)" the same issues.
[X 2023-06-01](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1664128466101633026)

His read on a famous case: "Twitter's biggest problem is a product strategy problem." [L2 27:42]

## You need to be more strategic: three different messages

He calls this "perhaps the most common feedback given to otherwise-competent PMs", and says it can mean three
different things. [X 2023-10-12](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1712262410764173598)

| Type | What it asks for | Usually aimed at (his view) |
|---|---|---|
| More product thinking | "You need to do more Product Thinking and less Project Thinking" [X 2023-10-12] | Senior PMs and group PMs aiming for director |
| New opportunity creation | Create the next business, not only optimise the current one | Director level |
| Actual product strategy | Clarify vision and strategy, and get the org aligned on it | Group PM to VP level operators |

He adds: "Product strategy and being strategic are not always the same thing." [X 2023-10-12] The detail of product vs
project thinking is in an image attached to that post, which we can't quote. **Our reading:** project
thinking asks whether the work is getting done; product thinking asks whether it is the right thing, for
whom, and whether it worked.

## When an exec says everything is a priority

Three possible causes, from [X 2025-08-08](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1953639774176391648): no clear
product strategy, so they prioritize everything that intuitively “sounds” important; no trust in the team's
motivation; or the Focusing Illusion (he cites Kahneman): "Nothing is as important as you think it is while
you are thinking about it". **Our reading:** fix the first one first.

## Neither beats the other

He notes that people's strong take on whether execution or strategy matters more "is based on whatever it is
that they happen to be better at." [X 2024-05-20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1792563082365128940)

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Write the symptom** in one sentence (for example: we missed three dates in a row).
2. **Test the four causes** in `templates/03-execution-diagnosis.md`: strategy, interpersonal, culture, skills.
3. **Run the band-aid test.** List fixes already tried. Two failed fixes is a strong hint the cause is
   elsewhere.
4. **If it's strategy,** write a one-pager (`templates/04-strategy-one-pager.md`) that names who it's for and
   how you'll win, not a list of projects.
5. **Repeat it.** For the next month, open every planning meeting by restating it, and check whether
   decisions reference it.

**Worked scenario (invented):** a 40-person company has two teams that keep blocking each other. Process
fixes (a shared standup, then a joint Jira board) both fade within weeks. The diagnosis: there is no written
choice between the self-serve segment and the enterprise segment, so each team optimises for a different
customer. One page that picks self-serve for the next two quarters ends most of the escalations.

**Failure modes (our reading):** calling a roadmap a strategy; writing the strategy once and never repeating
it; adding process to an interpersonal problem; assuming every problem is strategy when some really are
skills or infrastructure.

**Use it now:** `templates/03-execution-diagnosis.md` and `templates/04-strategy-one-pager.md`.
