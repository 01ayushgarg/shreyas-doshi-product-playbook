# 01 · The LNO framework: where your effort goes

Citations: `[L1 mm:ss]` = Lenny's Podcast 2022 · `[L2 mm:ss]` = Lenny's Podcast live 2024 ·
`[SS slug]` = his Substack · `[X date]` = his post on X. Full list in `SOURCES.md`.

> "All your tasks are not created equal." [L1 00:35:02]

## The three kinds of task

| Type | What it returns | In his words |
|---|---|---|
| **L · Leverage** | 10x to 100x | "when you put in a certain amount of effort, you get 10X or 100X in return in terms of impact" [L1 00:35:56] |
| **N · Neutral** | about 1x | "So you put in 1X and you get 1.1 X, those are neutral tasks." [L1 00:35:56] |
| **O · Overhead** | less than 1x | "in terms of impact, you get back a lot less than you actually put in" [L1 00:35:56] |

The shortest version: "L - Leverage >10X · N - Neutral ~1X · O - Overhead <1X"
[X 2024-04-26](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1783854725999567130)

He credits the origin: "I learned the ideas behind it a while ago via Elizabeth Grace Saunders' INO
Technique & then I modified it to fit product work." [X 2023-03-05](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1632200170799763456)

## The mistake it fixes

> "Many people who are ambitious or are perfectionists like myself by default treat each of these types of
> tasks the same way, and therein lies the problem." [L1 00:35:56]

The fix is matching effort to type: "for the L tasks, let my inner perfectionist shine" [L1 00:36:47], and do
N and O tasks well enough, fast.

## The same task can be any letter

> "The same type of activity can actually be either an L task or an N task or an O task." [L1 00:37:47]

His example: notes from a meeting can be O, or, when they will shape a decision, "just spend an hour or even
two hours perfecting those notes because it's an L task." [L1 00:39:23]

## Put the letter on the task

> "To put it in practice, I prefixed every task in my to-do list with L: or N: or O: depending on what type of
> task it was, so I could force myself to be intentional about my level of effort."
> [X 2022-02-12](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1492345187665776642)

## Why L tasks don't get done: fear, not time

> "PMs implicitly just deep down they know what their L tasks are." [L1 00:41:43]

> "Because deep down, they don't know if they can formulate a good strategy. So time becomes a convenient
> excuse for us." [L1 00:42:38]

> "Basically, I intentionally do N tasks and O tasks. I fill up my day with N and O tasks." [L1 00:43:46]

> "Pay attention to your fears because they're telling you something." [L1 00:45:40]

On beating it [SS on-beating-procrastination]: "recognize that it is happening because of some latent fear",
then "tell yourself that you can still take the first step even though this fear exists". And: "The fear
never goes away (definitely hasn't for me). But you do make more progress with practice."

His practical trick: "Nothing, for me, at least fights my procrastination for L tasks better than changing
the place from which I'm working." [L1 00:44:26]

## "No time" is usually something else

> "In Prod Mgmt, a lack of time is often *really* a lack of... Prioritization across teams · Focus on what's
> been prioritized · Clarity on strategy · Discipline to do things right · Courage to do the right thing ·
> Staffing for key roles · Support from management · Understanding of the PM role"
> [X 2022-02-14](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1493253710964617216)

> "I am so busy because I'm not making good product decisions." [L2 17:48]

> "You have signed up for even more work for a feature you should not have built in the first place. That's
> why we're busy." [L2 23:19]

For leaders, busyness is largely "(a) nonsense work you've created for yourself, and (b) work you can
delegate, if you have the courage." [X 2025-05-12](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1921986917945770217)

## From IC to leader

> "The LNO System was career-changing & life-changing for me as a senior IC PM. The Radical Delegation
> Framework was career-changing & life-changing for me as a PM leader." [X 2023-03-05]
