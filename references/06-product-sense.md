# 06 · Product sense and taste

## Why it matters

A product team makes hundreds of micro-decisions about the experience and its trade-offs, and he notes it is
impossible to validate each one with research and metrics: "That is why Product Sense is such a vital
skill." [X 2021-10-13](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1448097811912855553)

His definition: "the ability to make more correct product decisions, macro and micro, in the presence of
ambiguity." [SS influence-power-and-product-management]

It is not only a PM skill. In a post about who takes his course, he writes: "If you build products (regardless
of your role), Product Sense is the main skill to hone and the ceiling for this skill is way higher than you
think it is" [LI 2026-07-27](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreyasdoshi_latest-data-on-whos-taken-the-product-sense-activity-7487476890035699712-zB3R). (The post promotes his paid course; we use only its public text.)

## The five skills it's made of

In his 2026 essay he breaks product sense into five skills, each with a test question
[SS why-product-sense-is-the-only-product]:

| Skill | His test question (lightly shortened) |
|---|---|
| **Empathy** | Can you figure out what people need beyond what AI has already analysed? |
| **Simulation** | "can you identify future possibilities based on your understanding of the domain, the technology, the competition, your customers, your users?" [SS why-product-sense-is-the-only-product] |
| **Strategic thinking** | Can you identify which segments your product must resonate with, and what your differentiators will be? |
| **Taste** | Given several excellent options, can you pick the optimal one for your business goals and explain why? |
| **Creative execution** | Given the right insights and options, what unique features and solutions can you conceive that your competition cannot? |

> "Product Sense is simply a convenient label we can assign to the combination of the skills above."
> [SS why-product-sense-is-the-only-product]

(Only the essay's own text is used here. The essay links to an AI chat; the AI's lines are not quoted.)

From the live talk: "taste is about the ability to identify what is really good, without needing to see its
results." [L2 31:37] Taste is not cynicism: anybody can say everything is horrible. [L2 32:59]

Creative execution also appears in his high-agency thread as "an essential component skill of High Agency"
[X 2020-06-27](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276959718179651586), which links this chapter to `08-high-agency.md`.

## Be skeptical about why anyone would care

> "To go from good to great, the most important (and necessary) mindset shift is to be habitually skeptical
> about why the customer should bother to use (or pay for) such and such product or feature."
> [X 2023-03-31](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1641694978220118016)

## The one-sentence test

Before building, he simulates a conversation: a friend describes a problem, and your future customer
recommends your product. The friend asks what it does. The prompt is: "what is the one sentence that this
customer of my product says in response that is both factually accurate and compelling enough?" [PVP 16:40]
Then "the product work that we do over the next six months, or three months or 12 months, whatever it is, is
just about making that sentence actually true." [PVP 16:40]

## Lovable, not just viable

He likes the idea of a minimum lovable product (he says it isn't his idea) because "people translate minimum
viable product to basically they end up building what is a minimum unlovable product". [PVP 20:10] The
practical part: decide what you'll create significant value on, and what you'll intentionally underperform
on, including coming in below some table stakes in the first release. [PVP 22:00]

> "Just by building table stakes, you've not made it any easier for a customer to say yes and certainly
> you've not made it easier for the customer to say hell yes." [PVP 22:00]

## Know what you're really selling

> "You can simplify complex decisions, if you understand what you are *really* selling."
> [SS do-you-know-what-you-are-really-selling]

The post lists what several well-known companies “really” sell in one word each, and ends with his own: “I
sell clarity”. (The detailed reasoning is in a linked AI chat, which we don't quote.)

## Feedback is the easy part

> "Collecting feedback is the easiest, most mechanical part of the job here."
> [X 2023-06-13](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1668414275839471616)

The value comes from translating feedback into unique insights and making creative choices (same post). Most
feedback should be added to your internal model and then discarded, because trying to please everyone will
likely please no one. [SS everything-isnt-meant-for-everyone]

## Get one level down

> "The truth is one level down. Always." [SS get-to-the-core-of-the-thing]

A question he quotes from his Twitter days: "how does this make our users love Twitter more?" [L1 00:19:42]

## Common traps

- **Bias for building:** feeling "compelled to be in constant motion" instead of understanding the problem,
  domain and competitors first. Good review feedback names things like "we can build it, but can't sell it".
  [SS the-problem-with-product-reviews]
- **Blaming distribution:** the "fundamental cognitive bias" of product builders is believing the product is
  great and the problem is marketing and distribution. [SS the-fundamental-cognitive-bias-of]
- **Skipping the fundamentals:** deciding to build "without very clearly thinking through customer
  motivation, very clearly thinking through differentiation, very clearly thinking through a distribution
  approach." [L2 19:32]
- **Tools as a strategy:** "Tools have never been a significant source of alpha in product success and that
  is not changing with AI tools." [SS why-product-sense-is-the-only-product] Or, shorter: "AI won't save you
  from building the wrong thing." [LI 2026-08-17](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreyasdoshi_ai-wont-save-you-from-building-the-wrong-activity-7495087044105281536-NyeB)
- **Letting AI skip the path:** teams using AI for more answers and prototypes "are also bypassing the
  thought process (the path), atrophying essential product skills, inviting their own obsolescence." His fix
  is "a genuine commitment to your craft, as it always has been." [LI 2026-09-12](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreyasdoshi_this-is-an-important-point-in-product-too-activity-7504582447133802496-SdQT)

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Write the one sentence** a happy customer would say to a friend. If the team can't agree on it, stop.
2. **Score the five skills** against the plan (`templates/07-product-sense-review.md`): which question can
   you answer with evidence, and which with a guess?
3. **Split the spec** into lovable on (two or three things) and deliberately below par on.
4. **Ask the skeptic's question** for each feature: why would the customer bother?
5. **Check motivation, differentiation and distribution** before committing.

**Worked scenario (invented):** a team building a scheduling tool for clinics writes the sentence: *It fills
last-minute cancellations for you.* That forces three choices: be lovable on the waitlist auto-fill, be
below par on reporting and custom branding for launch, and target small clinics with one front desk. Two
planned features (calendar themes, an analytics tab) move out of the first release.

**Failure modes (our reading):** treating taste as personal preference; using “MLP” as a reason to polish
everything; collecting feedback without updating the model; writing a sentence that is compelling but not
yet true and then forgetting to make it true.

**Limits:** product sense is judgment, so this chapter can structure it, not supply it. No checklist replaces
the skills themselves.

**Use it now:** `templates/07-product-sense-review.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. For your next feature: why should the customer bother? Be skeptical.
2. Customer motivation, differentiation, distribution: is each one written down?
3. What did the last round of feedback change in your *model*, not just the backlog?
