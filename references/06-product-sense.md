# 06 · Product sense and taste

## Why it matters

> "A typical product team makes 100s of micro-decisions about the user experience & trade-offs. It is
> impossible to validate every one of these with user research, metrics & tactics. That is why Product Sense
> is such a vital skill." [X 2021-10-13](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1448097811912855553)

His definition: "the ability to make more correct product decisions, macro and micro, in the presence of
ambiguity." [SS influence-power-and-product-management]

## What it's made of

> "Product Sense is simply a convenient label we can assign to the combination of the skills above."
> [SS why-product-sense-is-the-only-product]

Two of the skills he names in that essay:
- **Simulation:** "can you identify future possibilities based on your understanding of the domain, the
  technology, the competition, your customers, your users?"
- **Taste:** "given a set of excellent recommendations, can you figure out which recommendation is optimal
  for your business goals and can you explain your thinking in ways others understand?"

And from the live talk: "taste is about the ability to identify what is really good, without needing to see
its results." [L2 31:37] Taste is not cynicism: "Anybody can just say, "Everything is horrible."" [L2 32:59]

## Be skeptical about why anyone would care

> "To go from good to great, the most important (and necessary) mindset shift is to be habitually skeptical
> about why the customer should bother to use (or pay for) such and such product or feature... Skepticism
> enables truth discovery." [X 2023-03-31](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1641694978220118016)

## Feedback is the easy part

> "Collecting feedback is the easiest, most mechanical part of the job here. The alpha really comes from
> *translating that feedback to unique insights* and from *making creative choices*."
> [X 2023-06-13](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1668414275839471616)

> "Most of that feedback should be reviewed, evaluated, added to your internal model, and then discarded."
> [SS everything-isnt-meant-for-everyone] Because "targeting everyone and hoping to please everyone will very
> likely please no one."

## Get one level down

> "The truth is one level down. Always." [SS get-to-the-core-of-the-thing]

> "In every product conversation, the framing decides the discussion." [SS get-to-the-core-of-the-thing]

A question he quotes from his Twitter days: "how does this make our users love Twitter more?" [L1 00:19:42]

## Common traps

- **Bias for building:** "we don't take the time to thoroughly understand the problem, domain, competitors and
  other determinants of product success, because we feel compelled to be in constant motion."
  [SS the-problem-with-product-reviews] The outcome: "we can build it, but can't sell it."
- **Blaming distribution:** the "fundamental cognitive bias" of product people, "My product is great, but it
  isn't successful because I have a marketing & distribution problem." [SS the-fundamental-cognitive-bias-of]
- **Skipping the fundamentals:** shipping "without very clearly thinking through customer motivation, very
  clearly thinking through differentiation, very clearly thinking through a distribution approach." [L2 19:32]

## Tools won't save you

> "Tools have never been a significant source of alpha in product success and that is not changing with AI
> tools." [SS why-product-sense-is-the-only-product]

> "Everything you do with your product originates in your thinking, so the best (and really the only) path to
> consistently build successful products is to greatly improve the quality of your thinking."
> [X 2026-10-05](https://x.com/shreyas/status/2107254835364135176)

**Checks to run:**
1. For your next feature: why should the customer bother? Be skeptical.
2. Customer motivation, differentiation, distribution: is each one written down?
3. What did the last round of feedback change in your *model*, not just the backlog?
