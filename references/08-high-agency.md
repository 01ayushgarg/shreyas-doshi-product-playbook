# 08 · High agency

His main source on this is a 20-part thread from 27 June 2020, cited below as `[X 2020-06-27, n/20]`
with a link to each post. Part 13 could not be retrieved, and parts 7 and 15 carry their detail in images,
which we can't quote.

## What it is

> "High agency is about finding a way to get what you want without waiting for conditions to be perfect or
> otherwise blaming the circumstances." [L1 01:11:55]

He credits the term to Eric Weinstein. [L1 01:11:55] In the thread he calls it "an attitude I've seen in
every successful product manager & leader I've known" [X 2020-06-27, 1/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956836856393728),
and describes what it looks like: "High Agency People either push through in the face of adverse conditions
or manage to reverse the adverse conditions to achieve goals"
[X 2020-06-27, 2/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956838840295425).

## Talent times agency

He crosses talent with agency [X 2020-06-27, 3/20 to 6/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956839612018688):

| | Low agency | High agency |
|---|---|---|
| **Highly talented** | “Frustrated Geniuses”: may have a success or two, but in the long run give in to “the system” | “Game Changers” for their teams and companies |
| **Less talented** | | “Go Getters”, some of whom become Game Changers through their agency |

On hiring: "When hiring, I prefer Go Getters over Frustrated Geniuses."
[X 2020-06-27, 5/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956842602541056) And the third factor is integrity:
"Never compromise on Integrity, not even for Game Changers."
[X 2020-06-27, 6/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956843361763328)

## How it sounds

His contrast of “Low Agency Bob” and “High Agency Alice” is in an image, but the post says what to notice:
for Bob, everything is an “other” problem, while everything Alice says comes from what she can control.
[X 2020-06-27, 7/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956844099948544) He has seen many talented PMs held
back because they are like Bob, and others do very well without fancy degrees because they are like Alice.
[X 2020-06-27, 8/20 to 9/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956846142586880)

## How to cultivate it, in his words

- **Circle of influence.** He points to the Circle of Influence and Circle of Concern framework from *The 7
  Habits of Highly Effective People*, which he has gone back to whenever he is tempted "to recede to a Low
  Agency mindset". [X 2020-06-27, 12/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276959712861237248)
- **Language.** "Observing and adjusting your language and self-talk is an important aspect of cultivating
  High Agency." [X 2020-06-27, 14/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276959714580951041)
- **Traits and skills.** He splits high agency into traits and skills (detail in an image), and says
  "Ownership Mindset is perhaps the most important of the 3 Traits."
  [X 2020-06-27, 15/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276959716489302018) Creative execution is one of the
  component skills, which he compares to the Indian idea of jugaad.
  [X 2020-06-27, 16/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276959718179651586)
- **It can be learned.** "It isn't inscrutable magic. You can learn it"
  [X 2020-06-27, 20/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276959722495635456)

On the 2022 podcast he names the same components: "Ownership mindset is one component of high agency", plus
creative execution and resilience. [L1 01:13:56]

## Needs judgment

> "Indeed, High Agency without Keen Judgment = Trainwreck" [X 2026-05-13](https://x.com/shreyas/status/2054620576346689844)

## His updated view, and the cost

In 2020 he wrote that some people are born or raised with high agency, and "It can also be developed later in
life." [X 2020-06-27, 1/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956836856393728) In 2026 he added two caveats:
that not everyone can develop it, and that it brings major professional upsides but "also creates chronic
anxiety in life situations you can't control". [X 2026-05-12](https://x.com/shreyas/status/2054227795988124132)

Earlier, he named another downside: high-agency people are wired to never blame other people or bad luck,
so they "internalize failures & nasty jolts as being on you even though you may have tried your very best."
[X 2022-09-23](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1573322882822053889)

A habit that balances it: view your triumphs in the third person (humility), your worries in the second
(perspective), and your problems in the first (agency).
[X 2021-10-01](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1444009936569061376)

In the Amplitude interview he pairs agency with acceptance: try to improve external factors "and we should
feel high agency towards making" those changes, but also be "very cognizant of how I can best manage the
current situation I'm in." [AMP 01:14:24]

## How to apply it (our reading)

1. **Write the blocker** you're currently waiting on, in one sentence.
2. **Rewrite it as Alice would:** start with “I can...” and name one action inside your control this week.
3. **Check judgment:** is the action the right thing, or just motion? Ask one person you trust.
4. **Check the cost:** is any failure you're carrying really yours? Use his 1st/2nd/3rd-person habit.
5. **When hiring,** listen for language that makes everything an “other” problem in answers about past setbacks, and never trade
   integrity for talent.

**Worked scenario (invented):** a PM says: *We can't launch until the data team builds the pipeline.* The
Alice version: *I can ship a manual CSV export to five pilot customers next week and learn whether they use
the report before the pipeline exists.* The judgment check: is that safe for customer data? If yes, it ships.

**Failure modes (our reading):** agency without judgment (his trainwreck); treating every setback as your
fault; using “high agency” to justify working around people instead of with them.

**Limits:** by his own 2026 update, not everyone can develop it, and it has a real personal cost.

**Checks to run:**
1. Where are you waiting for conditions to be perfect?
2. Is your agency paired with judgment, or is it just motion?
3. Which failure are you carrying that wasn't really yours?
