# 13 · The founder track

Most of his material is written for PMs. This chapter pulls together what he says that applies most
directly to founders and CEOs of early-stage companies, mainly from his 2021 Prime Venture Partners podcast,
whose host introduced its audience as early-stage entrepreneurs. Only his own turns are quoted.

His caveat first: "there is no one formula" for reaching product-market fit, and context matters most.
[PVP 02:45]

## 1. Solve the problem, not a problem

The pattern he keeps seeing: customers describe a problem, promise to use the product, the team builds it,
and at launch the customer says thanks, but they have other priorities right now. [PVP 02:45]

> "It is usually the case that Yes, you did solve a problem. But that problem was not in the stack rank of
> priorities and was not a very highly ranked problem." [PVP 02:45]

Businesses "can barely do one, two or three things in a given quarter or given half", so your problem has to
fit those top priorities for enough customers, at a price that gets you the revenue you need. His method:
the "customer problems stack rank". Before discussing your problem or solution, ask customers what their top
problems are. [PVP 02:45]

## 2. The fired-or-promoted test

For B2B products, products that "prevent the decision maker from getting fired, are going to get adopted very
quickly." Products that get the buyer promoted also get adopted fairly quickly, "but not as quickly as the
first one." [PVP 08:10]

A related tactic: find the moment your buyer's job changes. At Stripe, his team asked what a startup's newly
hired finance leader "need to prove, in order to show that value in the first year or two", and then how the
product fits those goals. [PVP 08:10]

## 3. One sentence, then make it true

Write the one sentence a customer would say to a friend that is "both factually accurate and compelling
enough", then spend the next months making it true. [PVP 16:40] See `06-product-sense.md`.

## 4. Lovable on a few things, below par on others

He prefers minimum lovable to minimum viable, which too often becomes "a minimum unlovable product".
[PVP 20:10] Customers will call feature after feature "an absolute requirement". Be "extremely discerning" about which
table stakes are truly needed, and put the saved time into differentiators. [PVP 22:00]

When founders ask how to do that with four or five people against well-funded incumbents, his answer is the
segment: "the smaller the target segment, you pick the greater ability to create that differentiation". It
"needs to be strategically chosen." [PVP 22:00]

## 5. Launches need three things clear

Before building, think through “customer motivation”, “differentiation” and "a distribution approach".
[L2 19:32] He warns that most teams instead decide based on whether an engineer is free. [L2 19:32] And beware
the founder's version of the cognitive bias he names: believing the product is great and only distribution is
the problem. [SS the-fundamental-cognitive-bias-of]

## 6. The CEO's strategy job

- **If the strategy is in your head, the team deadlocks.** Teams escalate every X-or-Y choice to the CEO, who
  then wonders why they can't be more self-sufficient. "It's actually a problem that you as the head of
  product, or you as the CEO, need to address for your team." [PVP 34:55]
- **Be the chief repeating officer.** Keep repeating the strategy in context with the mission, because people
  forget and new people join. [PVP 34:55]
- **Frameworks come second.** Prioritization frameworks don't help much without a strategy. [PVP 34:55]
- **Execution first, early.** "Most early stage teams should actually optimize for execution." [L1 00:52:23]
- **Tiny team, fast product:** if it isn't a “HELL YEAH!”, it's a no.
  [X 2021-09-06](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1434940492685266948)
- **You are at the impact level.** Founders and CEOs often think about impact while the team argues
  execution. [L1 00:47:58] Name the level (`03-impact-execution-optics.md`).

## 7. Know what you're really selling

"You can simplify complex decisions, if you understand what you are *really* selling."
[SS do-you-know-what-you-are-really-selling] **Our reading:** write it in one word, and use it to settle
feature debates.

## 8. Your first PM hire

What he gives you (chapters 09 and 10):
- The PM role in his words: define the product and orchestrate actions across the org to enable its success.
  [X 2022-01-03](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1477797445169860610)
- An MSN list before the job description, at most three bullets per letter, each one testable.
  [X 2023-05-02](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1653199507042234368)
- Coachable vs learnable vs intrinsic attributes. [X 2022-09-28](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1574919727926812672)
- Go Getters over Frustrated Geniuses, and integrity without exception.
  [X 2020-06-27, 5/20](https://x.com/shreyas/status/1276956842602541056)

**Our suggestion (not his):** before hiring, write which of your current product decisions you'll hand over
and which you'll keep. A first PM given only execution, while the founder keeps every product call, is set
up for the level mismatch in chapter 03.

## How to apply it: a founder's month (our reading)

| Week | Do | Template |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ask 8 to 10 customers for their top three problems, before pitching. Score your problem's rank. | `07-product-sense-review.md` |
| 2 | Write the one sentence, the lovable / below-par split, and the target segment. | `04-strategy-one-pager.md` |
| 3 | Pre-mortem the next launch, including go-to-market. | `02-pre-mortem-kit.md` |
| 4 | Re-plan the quarter by opportunity cost; restate the strategy at every planning meeting. | `05-opportunity-cost-review.md` |

**Worked number (invented):** of 10 customer interviews, your problem is in the top three for 3, ranked
fourth to sixth for 4, and not mentioned by 3. **Our reading:** 3 in 10 is a signal to narrow the segment to
whatever those 3 have in common, not to build for all 10.

**Failure modes (our reading):** taking “I'd use it” as validation; building every table stake an enterprise
prospect lists; a strategy presented once at an offsite; hiring a PM to fix what is really a missing strategy.
